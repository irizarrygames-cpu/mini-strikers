Mini Strikers — Privacy

Last updated 24 October 2026

Mini Strikers is a football game made by JMRI Consulting Services LLC, also known as Irizarry Games (irizarrygames.com). On this page, "we" means them. It explains, in plain words, what the game keeps, why, who helps run it, and how to get rid of it.

The short version

  • You can play without an account, and then nothing of yours leaves your device.
  • An account is a username and a password. No email, no real name, no age, no phone number.
  • No ads, no tracking, no analytics, no cookies. Nothing is sold or shared.
  • You can delete your account in the game at any time, and it is gone straight away.

Who makes it

Mini Strikers is made and run by JMRI Consulting Services LLC (Irizarry Games), a small game and app studio in the United States. They are responsible for the information described on this page. You can reach them at irizarrygames@gmail.com, and their website is irizarrygames.com.

Playing without an account

Nothing of yours leaves your device. Your progress — coins, players, what you have won — is stored in your own browser or in the app, and goes nowhere else. You can play the whole game this way except the online parts, which need an account so other players know who they are playing.

If you make an account

Making an account lets you play against other people and keep your progress across devices. To do that, the game stores:

  • The username you chose. Other players can see it during a match, on the leaderboards and in friends lists. Pick a name you are happy for people to see, and not your real name.
  • Your password, scrambled. It is put through PBKDF2 before it is stored, so what is kept cannot be turned back into your password — not by us, not by anybody who got hold of the file.
  • Your game progress. Coins, the country you play for, what you own, your record online (games played, won, drawn and lost, and goals), your rank, your World Cup run, and who your friends in the game are, including friend requests.
  • Sign-in keys. When you log in, the server gives your device a random key so you stay signed in. It is stored with the date it was made, and it stops working after 30 days or when you log out, whichever comes first.
  • The day the account was made.
  • A note of each coin pack you buy, if you buy any: which pack, the day, and the store's reference number for the purchase. This is so a purchase is never counted twice and never lost. No card number, no billing details.

That is the whole list. No email address, no real name, no age, no phone number, no address, no photographs, no device identifiers, and nothing from anywhere else on your device.

We use it only to run the game: to sign you in, keep your progress, match you with other players, put you on the leaderboards, and let you play with your friends. It is not used for anything else.

What stays on your device

The game keeps a few things in your browser's (or the app's) own storage on your device:

  • your progress, so you can play without the internet;
  • if you are signed in, your username and your sign-in key, so you do not have to log in every time;
  • a copy of the game itself, so it starts quickly and works offline.

This stays on your device and is not used to track you. The game uses no cookies. Logging out removes the sign-in key. Clearing the site's data in your browser, or uninstalling the app, removes all of it, including the progress of a game played without an account.

What the server sees when you play

Like every website, the game's server sees your device's internet address (its IP address) when it sends you the game. It uses that address for one thing: to slow down anybody trying lots of passwords or making lots of accounts at once. It is held only in the server's memory, forgotten within about ten minutes, and never saved with your account or in the database.

During an online match, your moves and the ready-made chat messages you pick go through the server to the other players in that match. Matches are not recorded; only the result is added to your record.

Buying coins

Coin packs are not on sale yet. When they are, this is how it works.

In the app, coins can be bought in packs for real money. This happens only through the App Store or Google Play: they take the payment, and the game never sees a card number or any billing detail. Before anything is bought, the game asks a grown-up to type a few numbers that are written out in words. Parents can also use the family controls built into their phone to approve or block purchases.

When you buy a pack, the game sends the store's reference for that purchase to its own server, which checks it with Apple or Google and then adds the coins to your account. Coins cannot be turned back into money. Refunds are handled by Apple or Google, not by the game. Buying coins needs an account, so the coins follow you to any device.

What it does not do

  • No advertising, and no advertising identifiers.
  • No analytics, no tracking, no third-party trackers of any kind.
  • No cookies.
  • Nothing is sold, rented or shared with anybody, and nothing is used to build a profile of you.
  • No location, no contacts, no camera, no microphone, no files.
  • No typed chat. Players can only send short ready-made messages.
  • The game loads nothing from other websites — no outside scripts, fonts or pictures — so playing it sends nothing to anybody but its own server.

Who can see it

Other players can see your username and the country you play for. On the leaderboards they can also see your rank, your rank points, your wins, how many games you have played and how many goals you have scored. Your friends in the game can also see whether you are online, waiting for a match or playing one. Nothing else is shown to anyone — never your password and never your sign-in key.

We would only ever hand information over to anyone else if the law made us.

Who helps run the game

A few companies provide the computers the game runs on. They handle information only so the game can work, and none of them is an advertising or analytics company.

  • Render runs the game's server, in the United States (Virginia).
  • Cloudflare sits in front of that server and passes the game's pages and messages along quickly and safely. It sees your IP address on the way through, as every network does.
  • Neon runs the database where accounts are kept.
  • Apple and Google take the payment if you buy coins in the app, and handle it under their own privacy rules.

Like nearly every web host, Render and Cloudflare may keep short technical records of requests (such as the time and IP address) to keep their services running and safe. If you email us, your message is kept in our email inbox, which is run by Google.

Where it is kept

Accounts are kept on the game's server and in its database, run by the companies above. The server is in the United States. If you play from another country, your account is kept and handled there.

How long it is kept

  • Your account: until you delete it. Deleting it removes it straight away.
  • Sign-in keys: 30 days at most, or until you log out.
  • Your IP address: about ten minutes, in the server's memory only.
  • Copies for accidents: the database company keeps short-term copies so the game can recover if something breaks. A deleted account drops out of those on its own when they roll over, after a short time.
  • Emails you send us: only as long as we need them to help you.

Keeping it safe

  • Everything between your device and the server travels over an encrypted connection (HTTPS).
  • Passwords are never stored. Only a scrambled version is kept, made with PBKDF2, a different random salt for each account and 150,000 rounds.
  • Sign-in keys are long random numbers, and they run out.
  • Trying lots of passwords or making lots of accounts is slowed down automatically.
  • Usernames are checked for inappropriate words before they are accepted.

No website can promise to be perfectly safe. That is one more reason the game keeps so little: there is no email, real name or payment detail to lose.

Deleting it

You can delete your account from inside the game: Settings → Delete Account. It asks for your password, and then the account and everything in it is removed from the server, your sessions are ended, and you come off other players' friends lists. It is immediate and it cannot be undone.

If you only want to start again without losing the account, Settings → Reset Save clears your progress and keeps the account.

You do not need the app to do this. The game also works in any web browser at mini-strikers.onrender.com: sign in there and delete the account the same way.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, laws such as the GDPR (in Europe and the UK) and privacy laws in US states such as California may give you rights over your information: to see it, get a copy, correct it, delete it, object to how it is used, or complain to your local data protection authority.

Most of it you can do yourself in the game: it shows you your coins, what you own, your record and your rank, and Settings lets you change your password or delete the account. If you have a question, get in touch at the address below.

We use your account information because you asked for an account and the game needs it to give you one, and the IP address to keep the game safe from abuse.

Children and families

Mini Strikers is for everyone, of every age. It is also made to be safe for children to play: it asks for no personal information at all beyond a username and a password, usernames are filtered for inappropriate words, and in a match players can only send short ready-made messages, never anything typed. There are no ads and no way for a stranger to message a child. The only link out of the game is the one to this page, and in the app it sits behind a grown-up check.

Children can play the whole game without an account. If a child makes one, we suggest a grown-up helps them choose a username that is not their real name. If you are a parent or guardian and want to remove your child's account, use Settings → Delete Account in the game or in any web browser, or get in touch at the address below.
